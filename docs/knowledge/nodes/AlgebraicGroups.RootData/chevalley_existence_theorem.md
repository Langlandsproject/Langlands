# Chevalley Existence Theorem

For every root datum
\[
\mathcal R=(X,\Phi,X^\vee,\Phi^\vee)
\]
there exists a connected reductive algebraic group \(G\) over an
algebraically closed field and a maximal torus \(T\subseteq G\) such that
the root datum of \((G,T)\) is isomorphic to \(\mathcal R\).

Together with the Chevalley isomorphism theorem, this gives the
classification of connected reductive groups over an algebraically closed
field by root data.

*Proof strategy.*  
One first constructs the semisimple part from the reduced root system using
Chevalley bases in a semisimple Lie algebra and the corresponding one-parameter
root subgroups satisfying the Chevalley commutator relations. The lattice
\(X\) specifies how the central torus is attached and which isogeny form of
the semisimple group is required.

The resulting group is generated by a torus with character group \(X\) and
root subgroups indexed by \(\Phi\), subject to the commutator relations
encoded by the root datum. The construction produces a connected reductive
group whose roots and coroots are exactly \((\Phi,\Phi^\vee)\). The
isomorphism theorem then proves uniqueness up to isomorphism.
