# BN-Pairs and Tits Systems

A **BN-pair** (or **Tits system**) is an abstraction of the Bruhat
decomposition of a reductive group. It is the axiomatic input from
which buildings are constructed.

## Definition

Let \(G\) be a group. A **BN-pair** for \(G\) consists of:

- subgroups \(B, N \subseteq G\);
- a set \(S \subseteq W := N / (B \cap N)\) of involutions
  ("simple reflections")

satisfying:

(BN1) \(G = \langle B, N \rangle\);

(BN2) \(B \cap N\) is normal in \(N\), and \(W = N/(B \cap N)\) is
generated by \(S\);

(BN3) For all \(s \in S, w \in W\): \(s B w \subseteq B w B \cup B s w B\);

(BN4) For all \(s \in S\): \(s B s^{-1} \not\subseteq B\).

The data \((G, B, N, S)\) is called a **Tits system**. The group \(W\) is
the **Weyl group**, and \((W, S)\) is automatically a **Coxeter
system**.

## The Bruhat decomposition

From the BN-pair axioms one derives:

\[
G \;=\; \bigsqcup_{w \in W} B w B
\]

(a disjoint union of double cosets). Each double coset is isomorphic
(as a \(B \times B\)-set) to \(B \times U_w\) where \(U_w\) is a
specific subgroup. The decomposition is the **Bruhat decomposition**
in its abstract form.

## Examples

### Reductive groups

For a split connected reductive group \(G\) over a field \(k\) with
Borel \(B\), maximal torus \(T \subseteq B\), normaliser \(N = N_G(T)\):

- \(B \cap N = T\);
- \(W = N_G(T)/T\) is the Weyl group;
- \(S\) = simple reflections (reflections through the walls of the
  fundamental Weyl chamber).

This is the **spherical BN-pair**, and the resulting building is the
**spherical building** of \(G\).

### \(\operatorname{GL}_n\)

For \(G = \operatorname{GL}_n(k)\), \(B\) the upper-triangular Borel,
\(N\) the monomial matrices, the Weyl group is \(W = S_n\), the
simple reflections are the adjacent transpositions \(s_i = (i, i+1)\).
The Bruhat decomposition becomes the LU decomposition of matrices
modulo permutation.

### \(p\)-adic reductive groups

For \(G\) a connected reductive group over a non-archimedean local
field \(F\), there is an **affine BN-pair**:

- \(B\) = Iwahori subgroup,
- \(N\) = normaliser of the maximal split torus,
- the resulting Weyl group is an **affine Weyl group**.

The resulting building is the **affine building** (= Bruhat-Tits
building) of \(G\). See
[[node:buildings.affine_weyl_groups|affine Weyl groups]]
and [[node:buildings.bruhat_tits_buildings|Bruhat-Tits
buildings]].

## Parabolic subgroups via BN-pairs

For each subset \(I \subseteq S\), the subgroup
\(P_I := B W_I B\) (where \(W_I = \langle I \rangle \le W\)) is a
**standard parabolic subgroup**. All subgroups containing \(B\) are of
this form (parabolic-Bruhat correspondence).

## Building construction

Tits's general theorem: every BN-pair canonically determines a
**simplicial complex** (the **building**) on which \(G\) acts, with
combinatorial properties controlled by the Coxeter system \((W, S)\).

- **Spherical building**: from a spherical BN-pair; the resulting
  simplicial complex is finite-dimensional and "spherical" in the
  topological sense (each apartment is a sphere triangulation).
- **Euclidean (affine) building**: from an affine BN-pair; apartments
  are Euclidean spaces tiled by the affine Weyl group.
