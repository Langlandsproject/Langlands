# Connected Subgroups Generate a Closed Connected Subgroup

Let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group and let \(\{G_i\}_{i \in I}\) be a
family of closed connected algebraic subgroups of \(G\). The subgroup
\(\langle G_i : i \in I\rangle\) generated by the \(G_i\) is a closed
connected algebraic subgroup of \(G\). Moreover, finitely many of the
\(G_i\) and a finite product of them already give the generated subgroup:
there exist \(i_1,\dots,i_r\) such that
\[
\langle G_i : i \in I\rangle
  = G_{i_1}G_{i_2}\cdots G_{i_r}.
\]

*Proof.*  
For any finite word \(G_{i_1}\cdots G_{i_r}\), the multiplication map
\[
G_{i_1}\times \cdots \times G_{i_r} \to G
\]
has connected image. The dimensions of the closures of such products are
bounded by \(\dim G\), so choose a word whose closure has maximal dimension.
Multiplying by any \(G_j\) cannot increase the closure; otherwise the maximal
choice was not maximal. Thus the maximal product is stable under left and
right multiplication by every \(G_j\), hence is the subgroup generated by the
family.

The chosen product is the image of an irreducible variety under a morphism,
so it is constructible and contains an open subset of its closure. Since it
is stable under translations by its elements, it equals its closure. It is
therefore closed and connected.
