# Jordan Decomposition in a Linear Algebraic Group

Let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group and \(g\in G\). There are unique
commuting elements \(g_s,g_u\in G\) such that
\[
g=g_sg_u=g_ug_s,
\]
where \(g_s\) is semisimple and \(g_u\) is unipotent in any faithful
linear representation of \(G\). The decomposition is functorial: for every
homomorphism \(f:G\to H\),
\[
f(g)_s=f(g_s),\qquad f(g)_u=f(g_u).
\]

*Proof.*  
Embed \(G\) as a closed subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\). In
\(\operatorname{GL}(V)\), ordinary multiplicative Jordan decomposition gives
unique commuting polynomial expressions \(g_s=P(g)\) and \(g_u=Q(g)\) with
\(g_s\) semisimple and \(g_u\) unipotent. The polynomials can be chosen from
the finite subalgebra generated by \(g\) and are intrinsic to \(g\).

Because \(G\) is Zariski closed and stable under the algebraic operations
that define these polynomial expressions, \(g_s\) and \(g_u\) belong to
\(G\). Uniqueness in \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\) implies uniqueness in \(G\).
If \(f:G\to H\) is a homomorphism, then \(f(g_s)\) and \(f(g_u)\) are
commuting semisimple and unipotent elements whose product is \(f(g)\), so
uniqueness gives the functorial identities.
