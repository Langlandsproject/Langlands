# Parabolic Subgroups

A **parabolic subgroup** is a closed subgroup of a reductive group
that contains a Borel subgroup. Parabolics interpolate between Borel
subgroups (minimal parabolics) and the full group, and they index the
**flag varieties** \(G/P\), which are the geometric scaffolding of
representation theory.

## Definition

Let \(G\) be a connected reductive group over a field \(k\). A closed
subgroup \(P \subseteq G\) is **parabolic** if \(P_{\bar k}\) contains
a Borel subgroup of \(G_{\bar k}\).

Equivalently, \(P\) is parabolic iff the quotient \(G/P\) is a
**projective variety**. (For an arbitrary closed subgroup, \(G/P\) is
only quasi-projective; properness over Spec \(k\) characterises
parabolic.)

## Standard parabolics

Fix a Borel \(B \supseteq T\) (\(T\) a maximal torus) and a set of
simple roots \(\Delta\). For each subset \(I \subseteq \Delta\), the
**standard parabolic**
\[
P_I \;=\; B \cdot W_I \cdot B \quad (\text{where } W_I = \langle s_\alpha : \alpha \in I \rangle)
\]
is a parabolic subgroup, and every parabolic containing \(B\) is of
this form.

Moreover, every parabolic of \(G\) is \(G\)-conjugate to a unique
standard parabolic.

## Levi decomposition

**Theorem.** Every parabolic \(P \subseteq G\) admits a semidirect
product decomposition
\[
P \;=\; L \ltimes R_u(P),
\]
where \(R_u(P)\) is the **unipotent radical** of \(P\), and \(L\) (the
**Levi subgroup**) is reductive. The decomposition is unique up to
conjugation by \(R_u(P)\); the Levi subgroups containing \(T\) are
parametrised by reflection subgroups of the Weyl group.

For the standard parabolic \(P_I\): \(L_I\) has root system
\(\Phi_I = \mathbb{Z} I \cap \Phi\), and \(R_u(P_I)\) is generated by
the positive roots **not** in \(\Phi_I\).

## Examples

### \(\operatorname{GL}_n\)

Parabolics of \(\operatorname{GL}_n\) are stabilisers of **partial
flags** \(0 = V_0 \subset V_1 \subset \cdots \subset V_k = k^n\).

The standard parabolics correspond to compositions of \(n\) (ordered
partitions): if \(n = n_1 + \cdots + n_k\), the standard parabolic is
the block upper-triangular subgroup with blocks of sizes
\(n_1, \dots, n_k\). The Levi is \(\mathrm{GL}_{n_1} \times \cdots \times \mathrm{GL}_{n_k}\);
the unipotent radical is the block-strict-upper-triangular subgroup.

### \(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}\) and \(\operatorname{SO}_n\)

Parabolics correspond to **isotropic flags** of subspaces of the
ambient vector space; the Levi has a "GL × Sp" or "GL × SO" form
depending on the dimensions.

## Geometry of the flag variety

The variety \(G/P\) is:

- **projective** and **smooth**;
- **rational** (in fact built from \(\mathbb{A}^1\)'s via Bruhat
  cells);
- decomposed into **Schubert cells** \(BwP/P\) for \(w \in W^I\)
  (representatives for \(W/W_I\)), of dimension \(\ell(w)\);
- the closures \(\overline{BwP/P}\) are **Schubert varieties**, key
  objects in geometric representation theory.

## Parabolic induction

For a representation \(\sigma\) of \(L\) (the Levi of \(P\)), the
**parabolically induced representation**
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_P^G(\sigma) \;=\; \{f : G \to V_\sigma : f(pg) = \sigma(p) f(g)\}
\]
is the fundamental construction in representation theory of reductive
groups:

- For \(G\) over a local field: produces principal series and their
  generalisations.
- For \(G\) over a finite field: gives Harish-Chandra induction, the
  starting point for Lusztig's classification.
- For \(G\) over a global field: Eisenstein series and the residual
  spectrum.

## Role in Langlands

- **Local Langlands** for non-tempered representations is organised by
  Levi subgroups via parabolic induction (the **Langlands
  classification**).
- **Constant terms** of automorphic forms are computed along parabolic
  subgroups; they record the cuspidal data.
- **Bernstein decomposition** of the category of smooth
  representations of \(G(F)\) is indexed by pairs \((M, \sigma)\) of
  Levis and supercuspidal representations.
- **Affine Hecke algebras** and **Iwahori-Hecke algebras** arise from
  the parabolic-Bruhat decomposition of \(G/B\) and parahoric
  structures in the affine setting.
