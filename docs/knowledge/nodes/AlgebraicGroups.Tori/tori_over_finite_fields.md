# Tori over Finite Fields

Take \(k = \mathbb{F}_q\). The absolute Galois group is procyclic,
\(\Theta = \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\), topologically generated by the
Frobenius \(\varphi : x \mapsto x^q\). By
[[node:tori.f_tori_galois_module_classification]] (in the cocycle
form [[node:tori.classification_by_cocycles]]):

**Rank-\(n\) tori over \(\mathbb{F}_q\) are in bijection with
conjugacy classes of elements of finite order in
\(\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})\).**

Indeed a continuous homomorphism
\(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})\) is
determined by the image \(F\) of \(\varphi\); continuity (open
kernel, [[node:tori.splitting_field]]) forces \(F\) to have finite
order, and conversely any finite-order \(F\) determines a continuous
homomorphism. Conjugacy of homomorphisms is conjugacy of \(F\).

The torus with Frobenius datum \(F\) of order \(d\) splits over
\(\mathbb{F}_{q^d}\), and its group of rational points has order
\[
\bigl|T(\mathbb{F}_q)\bigr| \;=\; \bigl|\det\nolimits_{X_*}(q\,F - 1)\bigr|,
\]
e.g. the norm-one torus of \(\mathbb{F}_{q^2}/\mathbb{F}_q\)
(datum \(F = -1\) in \(\operatorname{GL}_1\)) has \(q + 1\) points.

**Remarks.**

- Every torus over a finite field is quasi-split in the trivial sense
  (it is a torus), but more is true: by Lang's theorem
  ([[node:forms.lang_theorem_finite_fields]]) every torsor under a
  connected group is trivial, which is why the classification here
  has no second cohomological layer.
- This bijection underlies the parametrization of the maximal tori of
  a finite reductive group \(G(\mathbb{F}_q)\) by \(F\)-conjugacy
  classes in its Weyl group (Deligne–Lusztig theory); the case
  treated here is \(G = \mathbb{G}_m^{\,n}\).
