# Characterization of Diagonalizable Groups

For a linear algebraic group \(D\) over an algebraically closed field, the
following conditions are equivalent:

1. \(D\) is diagonalizable, i.e. is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of a
   diagonal torus.
2. The coordinate algebra \(k[D]\) has a basis consisting of characters of
   \(D\).
3. Every finite-dimensional rational representation of \(D\) is a direct sum
   of one-dimensional character spaces.

*Proof.*  
If \(D\subseteq \operatorname{GL}_n\) is diagonal, the coordinate functions
on \(D\) are generated by restrictions of diagonal characters. These
characters form a group, and the coordinate algebra decomposes into their
weight spaces; in fact the nonzero weight spaces are one-dimensional.

A character basis of \(k[D]\) makes every comodule over \(k[D]\) split into
weight spaces, because the coaction of a finite-dimensional representation
involves only finitely many basis characters. This gives complete
reducibility into one-dimensional representations.

Conversely, apply the representation criterion to a faithful
finite-dimensional representation of \(D\). It decomposes as a direct sum of
characters, so \(D\) embeds into the diagonal subgroup of
\(\operatorname{GL}(V)\).
