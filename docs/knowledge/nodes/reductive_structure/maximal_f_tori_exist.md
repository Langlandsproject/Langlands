# Maximal F-Tori Exist in Connected F-Groups

Let \(G\) be a connected linear algebraic group over a field \(F\).

If \(F\) is infinite, then \(G\) contains maximal tori defined over \(F\), and
\(G\) is generated by the Cartan subgroups \(C_G(T)\) of these maximal
\(F\)-tori. The same existence statement holds over finite fields by Lang's
theorem.

Consequently, every semisimple element of \(G(F)\) lies in a maximal
\(F\)-torus. If \(F\) is infinite and \(G\) is reductive, then \(G\) is
unirational over \(F\) and \(G(F)\) is Zariski dense in \(G\).

*Proof.*  
For nilpotent connected groups, the semisimple elements form the unique
maximal torus, and the uniqueness makes it Galois-stable, hence defined over
\(F\). For a non-nilpotent group, one uses centralizers of noncentral
semisimple elements in \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)(F)\). These centralizers are
proper connected \(F\)-subgroups, so induction on dimension gives maximal
\(F\)-tori inside them. The general case is reduced to the good case by an
isogeny.

The generation assertion follows because the closed subgroup generated by the
Cartan subgroups attached to maximal \(F\)-tori has full Lie algebra and is
therefore all of \(G\). A semisimple \(F\)-point lies in the \(F\)-defined
centralizer of itself, so the existence theorem applied to that centralizer
puts it in a maximal \(F\)-torus.

For reductive \(G\), Cartan subgroups are maximal tori. Since \(F\)-tori are
unirational and rational points on a unirational variety are dense over an
infinite field, a finite product of maximal \(F\)-tori dominating \(G\) gives
unirationality and density.
