# Rank-One Subgroups and SL₂-Embeddings

For each root \(\alpha \in \Phi(G,T)\) of a connected reductive group,
there is a canonical **rank-one subgroup** \(G_\alpha \subseteq G\)
through which the algebraic group \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) (or \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\))
embeds, carrying the standard root structure into the root system of \(G\).

## The rank-one subgroup \(G_\alpha\)

Define
\[
G_\alpha = \langle U_\alpha, U_{-\alpha} \rangle \subseteq G,
\]
the closed subgroup generated by the root subgroup \(U_\alpha \cong \mathbb{G}_a\)
and its opposite \(U_{-\alpha} \cong \mathbb{G}_a\).

**Theorem.** \(G_\alpha\) is a connected semisimple group of rank one, and
there is a surjective group scheme morphism
\[
\varphi_\alpha : \mathrm{SL}_2 \longrightarrow G_\alpha
\]
with central kernel \(\mu_2\) (if \(\alpha\) is not divisible in \(X^*(T)\))
or trivial kernel. Under \(\varphi_\alpha\):
\[
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & t \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \mapsto x_\alpha(t), \quad
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix} \mapsto x_{-\alpha}(t), \quad
\begin{pmatrix} u & 0 \\ 0 & u^{-1} \end{pmatrix} \mapsto \alpha^\vee(u).
\]

## The coroot as a cocharacter

The **coroot** \(\alpha^\vee \in X_*(T) = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_m, T)\) is
exactly the cocharacter obtained as the composite
\[
\mathbb{G}_m \hookrightarrow \mathrm{SL}_2 \xrightarrow{\varphi_\alpha} G,
\]
where the first map sends \(u \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} u & \\ & u^{-1} \end{pmatrix}\).

This gives a canonical identification:
\[
\alpha^\vee = (\varphi_\alpha)_*\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \\ & -1 \end{pmatrix}
\in X_*(T).
\]

## Weyl group elements from rank-one subgroups

For each root \(\alpha\), the element
\[
n_\alpha = x_\alpha(1)\, x_{-\alpha}(-1)\, x_\alpha(1) = \varphi_\alpha
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
\in N_G(T)(k)
\]
represents the simple reflection \(s_\alpha \in W(G,T) = N_G(T)/T\).
These Weyl group representatives satisfy the braid relations and generate
\(N_G(T)\) over \(T\).

## SL₂ vs PGL₂

Whether the morphism \(\varphi_\alpha\) factors through \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) or only
\(\mathrm{SL}_2\) depends on the isogeny type of \(G\) and the root \(\alpha\):

- If \(\alpha^\vee\) maps \(-1 \in \mathbb{G}_m\) to a non-trivial element of
  \(G\), then the kernel is trivial and \(\varphi_\alpha\) is an embedding
  of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) (isogeny of degree 1 onto \(G_\alpha\)).
- If \(\alpha^\vee(-1) = 1\), then \(\varphi_\alpha\) factors through
  \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\).

For the simply-connected form, \(\mathrm{SL}_2 \to G_\alpha\) is always an
isomorphism; for the adjoint form, \(\mathrm{PGL}_2 \to G_\alpha\) is always
an isomorphism.

## Relation to sl₂-triples

At the Lie algebra level, the differential of \(\varphi_\alpha\) sends
the standard \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-triple \(\{e, f, h\}\) to the triple
\(\{E_\alpha, E_{-\alpha}, H_\alpha\}\) in \(\mathrm{Lie}(G)\), which
is an \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-triple in the sense that
\[
[H_\alpha, E_\alpha] = 2 E_\alpha, \quad
[H_\alpha, E_{-\alpha}] = -2 E_{-\alpha}, \quad
[E_\alpha, E_{-\alpha}] = H_\alpha.
\]
This is the connection between the algebraic-group and Lie-algebra
theories of root systems.

## Uniqueness of \(\varphi_\alpha\)

The morphism \(\varphi_\alpha\) is not unique: it depends on the choice
of root subgroup parametrizations \(x_\alpha, x_{-\alpha}\). Changing
\(x_\alpha(t) \mapsto x_\alpha(c t)\) for \(c \in k^\times\) changes
\(\varphi_\alpha\) by a \(T(k)\)-conjugation. A **pinning** fixes a
canonical choice of \(x_\alpha\) for each simple root, making
\(\varphi_\alpha\) canonical for simple roots.
