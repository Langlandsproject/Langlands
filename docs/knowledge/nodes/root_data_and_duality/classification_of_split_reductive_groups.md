# Classification of Split Reductive Groups

The **Chevalley-Demazure-Grothendieck theorem** classifies split
connected reductive groups by their based root data. This is the
foundational theorem of the structure theory, putting the entire zoo
of classical and exceptional groups onto a uniform footing.

## Statement

**Theorem (Chevalley 1955; Demazure-Grothendieck SGA3, 1962-64).** Let
\(k\) be a field. The map
\[
\{\text{split connected reductive } k\text{-groups}\}/\cong \;\longrightarrow\; \{\text{based root data}\}/\cong
\]
\[
(G, T, B) \;\longmapsto\; (X^*(T), \Phi, X_*(T), \Phi^\vee; \Delta)
\]
is a **bijection** between isomorphism classes of split connected
reductive groups (with chosen pinning) and isomorphism classes of
based root data.

Moreover, every based root datum lifts to a **Chevalley group scheme**
\(G_{\mathbb{Z}}\) defined over \(\mathbb{Z}\), unique up to
isomorphism, with \(G = G_{\mathbb{Z}} \times_{\mathbb{Z}} k\) the
split form over \(k\).

## Pinned form of the theorem

A **pinning** of a split reductive group \((G, T, B)\) is a choice of
non-zero root vector \(X_\alpha \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha\) for each
simple root \(\alpha \in \Delta\). The pinning rigidifies the group:
the **pinned automorphism group** \(\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{pin}}(G, T, B, \{X_\alpha\})\)
equals the automorphism group of the based root datum.

Without pinning, one has an exact sequence:
\[
1 \;\to\; G_{\mathrm{ad}}(k) \;\to\; \mathrm{Aut}(G) \;\to\; \mathrm{Out}(G) \;\to\; 1,
\]
where \(\mathrm{Out}(G) = \mathrm{Aut}(\text{based root datum})\) is
the group of diagram automorphisms.

## Existence: Chevalley's construction

For each based root datum, Chevalley constructed the corresponding
split group via:

1. Choose a complex semisimple Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\)
   with the given root system.
2. Choose a **Chevalley basis** \(\{X_\alpha, H_\alpha\}\) of
   \(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\) with integer structure constants.
3. Define \(G_{\mathbb{Z}}\) as the group scheme generated by the
   formal "exponentials" \(\exp(t X_\alpha)\) and the maximal torus,
   subject to the Steinberg relations.

The resulting group scheme works over any commutative ring.

## Uniqueness: the isomorphism theorem

**Theorem.** If \((G, T, B)\) and \((G', T', B')\) are split connected
reductive groups over \(k\) with isomorphic based root data
\((X, \Phi, X^\vee, \Phi^\vee; \Delta) \cong (X', \Phi', \ldots; \Delta')\)
via some isomorphism \(\phi\), then there exists an isomorphism
\(f : G \to G'\) inducing \(\phi\) on root data. The choice of \(f\)
is unique up to composition with conjugation by \(T(k)\).

## Examples of classification

| Based root datum | Group |
|---|---|
| \(A_{n-1}\), \(X = \mathbb{Z}^n\) | \(\mathrm{GL}_n\) |
| \(A_{n-1}\), \(X = P\) (weight lattice) | \(\mathrm{SL}_n\) |
| \(A_{n-1}\), \(X = Q\) (root lattice) | \(\mathrm{PGL}_n\) |
| \(B_n\) sc | \(\mathrm{Spin}_{2n+1}\) |
| \(B_n\) ad | \(\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}\) |
| \(C_n\) sc | \(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}\) |
| \(D_n\) sc | \(\mathrm{Spin}_{2n}\) |
| \(E_6\) sc | \(E_6^{\mathrm{sc}}\) (78-dim Lie group) |
| \(G_2\) (= sc = ad) | the exceptional group \(G_2\) |

## Working over \(\mathbb{Z}\)

The Chevalley group scheme \(G_{\mathbb{Z}}\) has fibres
\(G_{\mathbb{Z}} \times_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{F}_p = G_p\) over each
prime, all of which are "the same group in characteristic \(p\)"
(modulo bad-prime subtleties). This makes the classification work
universally:

- **\(G(\mathbb{F}_q)\)**: finite group of Lie type, with order
  given by Steinberg's formula in terms of the root system;
- **\(G(\mathbb{Q}_p)\)**: \(p\)-adic group of Lie type;
- **\(G(\mathbb{C})\)**: complex Lie group;
- **\(G(\mathbb{R})\)**: real Lie group (split form).

## Non-split forms

Once the classification of split forms is fixed, non-split forms over
a field \(k\) are classified by **Galois cohomology of the
automorphism group**:
\[
\{\text{forms of split } G \text{ over } k\} \;\cong\; H^1(k, \mathrm{Aut}(G)).
\]

The exact sequence
\(1 \to G_{\mathrm{ad}} \to \mathrm{Aut}(G) \to \mathrm{Out}(G) \to 1\)
gives a decomposition into inner forms (in \(H^1(k, G_{\mathrm{ad}})\))
and outer forms (Galois-equivariant pinned-automorphism cocycles).

## Role in Langlands

- **Langlands functoriality**: requires the classification — to
  speak of \({}^L G\), one needs the dual group \(\hat G\), which is
  defined by reversing the root datum. The classification is what
  makes this dual group well-defined and unique.
- **Reductive group schemes (SGA3)**: the existence of
  \(G_{\mathbb{Z}}\) enables the global Langlands programme on
  Shimura varieties at all primes simultaneously.
- **Categorification**: the geometric Satake equivalence
  \(\mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G) = \mathrm{Rep}(\hat G)\) is a
  "categorification" of the classification.
