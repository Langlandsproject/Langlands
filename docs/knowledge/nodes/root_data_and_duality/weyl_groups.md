# Weyl Groups

The **Weyl group** \(W\) of a root system (or root datum, or reductive
group) is the finite group generated by the reflections through the
roots. It is the symmetry group of the combinatorial structure of the
group and controls almost every refined invariant: character formula,
Bruhat decomposition, Hecke algebra, geometry of the flag variety.

## Definition (abstract)

Let \(\Phi \subseteq V\) be a root system in a Euclidean space \(V\).
The **Weyl group** of \(\Phi\) is the subgroup
\[
W \;=\; W(\Phi) \;\subseteq\; O(V)
\]
generated by the reflections
\[
s_\alpha(x) \;=\; x - \langle x, \alpha^\vee \rangle \alpha, \qquad \alpha \in \Phi.
\]

By the root-string axioms, \(W\) permutes \(\Phi\); since \(\Phi\) is
finite, \(W \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Sym}(\Phi)\) is finite.

## Simple reflections and Coxeter presentation

Fix a base \(\Delta = \{\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_r\}\) and let
\(s_i = s_{\alpha_i}\). Then:

- \(W\) is generated by \(\{s_1, \dots, s_r\}\) (the **simple
  reflections**).
- The relations are:
  \[
  s_i^2 = 1, \qquad (s_i s_j)^{m_{ij}} = 1
  \]
  where \(m_{ij}\) is the order of \(s_i s_j\), determined by the angle
  between \(\alpha_i\) and \(\alpha_j\):
  \[
  m_{ij} \in \{2, 3, 4, 6\} \quad \text{for } i \ne j.
  \]

Thus \((W, \{s_i\})\) is a **Coxeter system**.

## Definition (from a reductive group)

For a connected reductive group \(G\) over a field \(k\) with maximal
torus \(T\) and normaliser \(N_G(T)\):
\[
W(G, T) \;=\; N_G(T) / T.
\]

**Theorem.** For \(G\) split, the abstract Weyl group of the root
datum and the geometric Weyl group \(N_G(T)/T\) are canonically
isomorphic.

For \(G\) non-split, the **rational Weyl group** is
\(W(G, T)(k) = (N_G(T)/T)(k)\), often a proper subgroup of the
absolute one and equal to the \(\Gamma\)-invariants of the absolute
Weyl group.

## Length function and Bruhat order

For \(w \in W\), the **length** \(\ell(w)\) is the minimum number of
simple reflections needed to write \(w = s_{i_1} \cdots s_{i_\ell}\).
Equivalently:
\[
\ell(w) \;=\; \#\{\alpha \in \Phi^+ : w(\alpha) \in \Phi^-\}.
\]

The **Bruhat order** \(\le\) on \(W\) is generated by:
\[
v \le w \iff v \text{ can be obtained from } w \text{ by deleting letters in some reduced expression}.
\]

\((W, \le)\) is a finite, ranked, self-dual poset with rank function
\(\ell\). The Hasse diagram of \((W, \le)\) is the **Bruhat order
diagram**.

## The longest element

There is a unique element \(w_0 \in W\) of maximal length
\(\ell(w_0) = \#\Phi^+\). It satisfies \(w_0^2 = 1\) and
\(w_0(\Phi^+) = \Phi^-\). For \(\mathrm{GL}_n\),
\(w_0 = (n, n-1, \dots, 1)\) (the reverse permutation).

## Affine Weyl group

For a reductive group over a local field, the **affine Weyl group**
is
\[
W_{\mathrm{aff}} \;=\; W \ltimes \mathbb{Z}\Phi^\vee,
\]
the semi-direct product of \(W\) with the coroot lattice. It is the
Weyl group of the affine root system.

The **extended affine Weyl group** \(\tilde W = W \ltimes X_*\)
includes all translations by cocharacters.

## Role

- **Bruhat decomposition.** \(G = \sqcup_{w \in W} BwB\); cells
  indexed by \(W\), dimensions by \(\ell\).
- **Schubert calculus.** Cohomology of \(G/B\) has basis indexed by
  \(W\); intersection products encoded by **Schubert structure
  constants**.
- **Character formula.** Weyl character formula uses the alternating
  sum over \(W\) and the longest element \(w_0\).
- **Hecke algebras.** Iwahori–Hecke algebra \(\mathcal{H}(W)\) is a
  deformation of the group algebra \(\mathbb{Z}[W]\), foundational for
  Kazhdan–Lusztig theory.
- **Demazure operators.** Operators \(\partial_{s_i}\) on the
  character ring, used to construct irreducible characters and
  resolutions of Schubert varieties.

## Mathlib coverage and formalization gaps

**Present in Mathlib4:**
- `RootPairing.weylGroup` — the Weyl group defined as the subgroup of
  `Aut(M)` generated by the simple reflections `{s_i}`. Located in
  `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.RootSystem.WeylGroup` and related modules.
- **Permutation representation**: The action of `weylGroup` on the root
  index set `ι` (via `reflectionPerm`) is proved to give a group
  homomorphism `weylGroup →* Sym(ι)`.
- `CoxeterSystem` — an independent abstract Coxeter group framework in
  `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Coxeter.Basic`, with generators and Coxeter
  relations defined abstractly.

**Formalization gaps (explicit TODOs):**
- **Finiteness**: The statement "`RootPairing.weylGroup` is finite" is an
  explicit TODO. The proof would use the embedding into `Sym(ι)` (since the
  root index set `ι` is finite for a finite root pairing), but this
  embedding is not yet established.
- **Faithfulness**: The permutation representation `weylGroup →* Sym(ι)` is
  not yet proved to be injective (faithful). This is an explicit TODO.
- **No connection to `CoxeterSystem`**: There is currently no theorem in
  Mathlib4 connecting `RootPairing.weylGroup` to `CoxeterSystem`. The
  isomorphism between the Weyl group (defined geometrically from the root
  pairing) and the abstract Coxeter group (defined by the Coxeter matrix
  from the Dynkin diagram) is not formalized.
- **Matsumoto's theorem** (any two reduced expressions for \(w\) are related
  by braid relations) is stated as a TODO in
  `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Coxeter.Basic`.

For a detailed discussion of these gaps and the Coxeter presentation
theorem, see
[[node:root_data_and_duality.weyl_group_coxeter_structure|Weyl Groups as Coxeter Groups]].
