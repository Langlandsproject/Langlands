# Conjugacy Classes in the Weyl Group

## Setup

Let $G$ be a connected reductive group with Weyl group $W = N_G(T)/T$.
As an abstract group, $W$ is a finite Coxeter group generated by simple
reflections $\{s_\alpha : \alpha \in \Delta\}$.

## Classification (Carter)

The conjugacy classes of $W$ are classified by **admissible diagrams**: certain
subgraphs $\Gamma$ of the extended Dynkin diagram $\tilde{\Delta}$, subject to
the condition that the corresponding sub-root-system decomposes as a product
of root systems of types $A$, $D$, or $E$ only (no $B$, $C$, $F$, $G$).

Each admissible diagram $\Gamma$ gives a conjugacy class $C_\Gamma \subset W$;
its representative is the product of simple reflections for the nodes of $\Gamma$.

## Elliptic elements

An element $w \in W$ is **elliptic** if it has no fixed vector in the real
reflection representation, i.e.\ $V^w = 0$ where $V = X^*(T) \otimes \mathbb{R}$.
Equivalently, $w$ is not contained in any proper parabolic subgroup of $W$.

Elliptic elements play a key role in:
- The **elliptic representation theory** of $p$-adic groups (depth-zero
  supercuspidal representations).
- The **elliptic pairing** on class functions: $(f, g)_{\mathrm{ell}} =
  |W|^{-1}\sum_{w \text{ ell}} f(w)g(w^{-1})$.

## Connection to Springer correspondence

Under the Springer correspondence:
$$\bigsqcup_{\mathcal{O}} \mathrm{Irr}(A(\mathcal{O})) \xleftrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
\widehat{W},$$
each irreducible $W$-representation $\phi_w$ (attached to a conjugacy class
$C_w \subset W$) corresponds to a pair $(\mathcal{O}, \mathcal{L})$. The
**Springer character** $\chi_w : W \to \mathbb{Z}$ encodes which unipotent
orbit $\mathcal{O}$ is attached to $C_w$.

## Connection to Kazhdan–Lusztig theory

Conjugacy classes in $W$ parametrise **cells** in the Kazhdan–Lusztig cell
decomposition of $W$: two-sided cells $\mathbf{c}$ of $W$ biject with special
unipotent orbits, and the number of left cells within $\mathbf{c}$ equals
the number of irreducible representations of $W$ attached to $\mathcal{O}_\mathbf{c}$.

## Number of conjugacy classes by type

| Type | $|W|$ | \# classes |
|---|---|---|
| $A_n$ | $(n+1)!$ | $p(n+1)$ (partitions) |
| $B_n / C_n$ | $2^n n!$ | bipartitions of $n$ |
| $D_n$ | $2^{n-1} n!$ | bipartitions of $n$ minus pairs |
| $G_2$ | 12 | 6 |
| $F_4$ | 1152 | 25 |
| $E_6$ | 51840 | 25 |
| $E_7$ | 2903040 | 60 |
| $E_8$ | 696729600 | 112 |
