# AI4Math 3-5 Day Formalization Project Ledger

Date: 2026-07-16

## Purpose

This ledger records theorem-sized Lean projects extracted from the Langlands formalization
roadmap. Each project must be achievable by students in three to five days with AI assistance.
The project is accepted only after its Mathlib entry points and a representative target statement
have been checked in a scratch Lean file.

Status values:

- `candidate`: mathematically plausible, not yet compiled;
- `validated`: representative declarations compile and the remaining work is bounded;
- `rejected`: too large, blocked, already trivial, or based on a false/unsafe statement.

## Validation Protocol

For every candidate:

1. locate exact Mathlib declarations and inspect their types;
2. write the smallest representative declaration in `/tmp`, outside the repository;
3. compile it with `lake env lean` from `lean/`;
4. separate required deliverables from stretch goals;
5. test for hidden dependencies on exchange, Jordan classification, algebraic-group geometry, or
   missing quotient infrastructure;
6. record the result below before creating a student-facing GitHub issue.

## Coxeter Candidates

### C1. Standard Parabolic Core

- Status: `validated`
- Target: define the subgroup generated by selected simple reflections and prove monotonicity,
  empty, full, and generator-membership lemmas.
- Mathlib start: `CoxeterSystem.simple`, `CoxeterSystem.subgroup_closure_range_simple`, and the
  generic subgroup-closure/order APIs. The exact namespace and theorem names for monotonicity and
  the empty closure must be checked in scratch; do not promise `Subgroup.closure_mono` or
  `Subgroup.closure_empty` without that check.
- Required deliverable: one definition and four lemmas.
- Explicit exclusions: cosets, Coxeter faces, incidence, chambers, and Bruhat order.
- Expected risk: low; the `Set.image`/`Set.range` rewrite for the full subset is the main API check.
- Validation: the definition, monotonicity proof via `Subgroup.closure_le`, empty case, and
  generator-membership theorem compile in the isolated probe.

### C2. Inversion Sequences as Finsets

- Status: `validated`
- Target: turn `rightInvSeq` and `leftInvSeq` of a reduced word into finsets; prove their
  cardinalities equal the word length and every member satisfies the corresponding inversion
  predicate.
- Mathlib start: `CoxeterSystem.rightInvSeq`, `leftInvSeq`,
  `IsReduced.nodup_rightInvSeq`, `IsReduced.nodup_leftInvSeq`,
  `isRightInversion_of_mem_rightInvSeq`, `isLeftInversion_of_mem_leftInvSeq`.
- Required deliverable: two definitions and both left/right cardinality and membership theorems.
  The project is an API bridge, not a new Coxeter theorem; it is suitable only if the student also
  supplies clean simp/extensionality lemmas and examples.
- Explicit exclusion: equality with the set of all inversions, whose reverse inclusion needs
  exchange-level input.
- Expected risk: low to medium; finite decidable equality assumptions must be scoped honestly.
- Validation: both `List.toFinset` definitions, the cardinality proof via
  `CoxeterSystem.IsReduced.nodup_rightInvSeq` and `length_rightInvSeq`, and the matrix/Lie imports
  compile in the isolated probe.

### C3. A Braid Move Preserves Word Product

- Status: `validated`
- Target: define a local braid move inside arbitrary prefix/suffix contexts and prove that it
  preserves `wordProd`.
- Mathlib start: `CoxeterSystem.wordProd_append`, `CoxeterSystem.wordProd_braidWord_eq`,
  `CoxeterSystem.braidWord`.
- Required deliverable: one contextual inductive relation, its reflexive-symmetric-transitive
  closure, and product-invariance theorems for both the one-step relation and the closure.
- Stretch: small rank-two examples using `I₂ₘ`.
- Explicit exclusion: Matsumoto's theorem and any converse.
- Expected risk: low to medium; orientation and symmetry must be stated correctly. Without the
  contextual relation and its closure this is too shallow for a standalone three-day project.
- Validation: the contextual inductive relation, `Relation.EqvGen` closure, and both product
  invariance proofs compile using `wordProd_append` and `wordProd_braidWord_eq`.

### C4. Executable Finite Coxeter Matrix Tests

- Status: `rejected`
- Target: prove entry formulas and fixed-rank executable examples for `A`, `B`, `D`, and
  dihedral matrices; record that type `C` uses the same Coxeter matrix as type `B`.
- Mathlib start: `CoxeterMatrix.Aₙ`, `Bₙ`, `Dₙ`, `I₂ₘ`, `G₂`.
- Required deliverable: a small theorem/test suite, not a new classification.
- Explicit exclusion: general `D₃`/`A₃` reindex equivalence unless an explicit permutation is
  already easy to compile.
- Rejection reason: the proposed formulas and fixed-rank tests are mostly `simp`, `rfl`, or
  `native_decide`; the claimed `C_n` equality is underspecified because Mathlib has no `Cₙ` object
  and the equality depends on rank/index conventions. Keep such tests as examples inside another
  project, not as a student formalization project.

## Nilpotent Candidates

### N1. The Standard Matrix sl2-Triple

- Status: `validated`
- Target: define a small `SL2Triple` predicate or structure and verify the standard matrices
  \(e,h,f\in M_2(K)\); prove `e` and `f` nilpotent and derive nilpotence of their adjoint maps.
- Mathlib start: `Mathlib.Algebra.Lie.Matrix`, matrix commutator Lie bracket,
  `LieAlgebra.ad_nilpotent_of_nilpotent`.
- Required deliverable: all three triple relations plus nilpotence of `e`, `f`, and at least one
  explicitly named adjoint map.
- Explicit exclusions: trace-zero Lie subalgebra packaging, Jacobson-Morozov existence or
  uniqueness, and conjugacy classification.
- Expected risk: low to medium; matrix extensionality and characteristic assumptions need scratch
  validation.
- Validation: the standard matrices, all three bracket relations, nilpotence of `e` and `f`, and
  nilpotence of `ad e` compile in the isolated probe over a generic commutative ring. The proof
  emits only non-fatal tactic/linter warnings.

### N2. Nilpotent Does Not Mean ad-Nilpotent Conversely

- Status: `rejected`
- Target: package the existing implication from nilpotent elements of an associative matrix
  algebra to nilpotent adjoint maps, then exhibit a nonzero scalar matrix with zero adjoint map to
  refute the converse in a matrix Lie algebra.
- Mathlib start: `LieAlgebra.ad_nilpotent_of_nilpotent` for an element of an associative algebra,
  `LieAlgebra.isNilpotent_ad_of_isNilpotent` for a Lie subalgebra of that algebra,
  `LieDerivation.ad_ker_eq_center`, and matrix Lie algebra instances.
- Required deliverable: one forward theorem wrapper and one explicit counterexample.
- Explicit exclusions: an unconditional theorem for arbitrary Lie algebras, Engel's theorem, and
  a center-free converse unless it becomes immediate from an existing injectivity result.
- Rejection reason: Mathlib already contains the forward theorem and the adjoint-kernel theorem;
  a wrapper plus the identity-matrix counterexample is too small for three to five days. A larger
  center/kernel theorem could be reconsidered only after a concrete matrix-center gap is found.

## Rejected or Deferred

### R1. Full Standard Parabolics and Coxeter Complex

- Status: `rejected`
- Reason: quotient/coset equality, reverse-inclusion face order, incidence, and group actions are
  several projects, not a three-to-five-day task.

### R2. Matsumoto Theorem or General Bruhat Order

- Status: `rejected`
- Reason: Mathlib explicitly lacks Matsumoto; expression independence and partial-order proofs
  require exchange/deletion infrastructure.

### R3. Chosen-Position Subword Poset

- Status: `deferred`
- Reason: mathematically safe, but dependent indexing and ordered extraction may consume most of
  the project window without advancing the Langlands-facing API.

### R4. Linear Nilpotent Cone with Full Conjugation Action

- Status: `deferred`
- Reason: preservation of nilpotence is small, but packaging the action, orbit equivalence, and
  stabilizer/centralizer description is too broad for the current time limit. It may be split into
  a single conjugation-invariance theorem after scratch validation.

### R5. Partitions to Jordan Blocks or Nilpotent-Orbit Classification

- Status: `rejected`
- Reason: Mathlib has partitions and block matrices but no Jordan-block/Jordan-normal-form API.
  Dependent block indexing and classification are too large.

### R6. Jacobson-Morozov, Bala-Carter, Springer Theory, or Scheme-Level Nilpotent Cone

- Status: `rejected`
- Reason: these require missing reductive-group, Lie-of-group, orbit geometry, or representation
  infrastructure and cannot produce an honest three-to-five-day Lean deliverable.
